400 mm beam wall mount engineering report v0
Design baseline and analysis record    17 November 2026
This version defines a 400 mm purchased aluminum tube supported between two opposite walls by identical printed PETG end brackets. It includes reproducible CAD, hand calculations, executed finite element analyses, purchase evidence, a priced bill of materials and automated checks. The report supports review of the design and the next qualification steps.
Numerical verification: FAIL. Principal stress changes 14.44% against a 10% limit; beam mesh volume error is 0.5032% against 0.5%. Stress magnitudes remain below the material screening thresholds. Physical qualification is BLOCKED. Engineering release is not approved.

Rounded square section formulas
At nominal dimensions, A = 437.023 mm² and I = 88,514.405 mm⁴. Independent numerical integration and FreeCAD volume inertia are checked against these formulas. The script enumerates the dimensional tolerance corners: outside width ±0.635 mm, wall ±0.2032 mm and radius ±0.396875 mm [S2].
The resulting conservative values are Imin = 78,669.225 mm⁴, Amax = 474.481 mm² and Z = Imin/cmax = 4,061.919 mm³. Independently extreme dimensions are combined deliberately; actual dimensions must still be measured.
Beam force stress and deflection calculations
The calculation span is L = 400.5 mm, including the positive cut tolerance. The force is F = 10 N. All maximum tube weight is assigned conservatively to one bracket for its load envelope; the beam bending model uses the uniformly distributed tube weight W = 5.220 N.
Substitution gives Mmax = 10 × 400.5/4 + 0.013033 × 400.5²/8 = 1,262.563 N mm. The point force at midspan maximizes simply supported bending. A transverse end moment envelope also covers restraint from snug plugs; perfect pins are not assumed for the bracket check.
The diagonal section factor bounds transverse direction; axial force at a section corner contributes Me = 273.898 N mm. Calculated bending stress is 0.535 MPa. This envelope intentionally combines some loads that cannot all occur simultaneously.
The torsion term is a closed thin wall screening approximation. Combined stress is 0.625 MPa against 240/3 = 80.000 MPa. The ratio of material reference yield to this stress is 383.7.
The computed global beam deflection is 0.00428 mm against the added 1 mm target. The interface contact model on the next page gives a maximum local force of 100 N. A 10 mm axial wall strip under that force gives 55.694 MPa against the 80 MPa screen. Smaller or sharp contact footprints require another local model. These values exclude installation movement and long term creep.
For comparison with the centered vertical FEM case, use the nominal section and L = 400 mm, with Me = 0. The first two deflection terms then give 0.00284043 mm. Compare this global bending quantity with a section displacement, while retaining the local maximum separately.
Global FEM displacement 0.00307321 mm averages 101 sidewall nodes with |x − midspan| ≤ h/2, |z| ≤ 2 mm and |y| ≥ 0.4 OD. The difference from Euler is 8.195%, within 15%. The 0.00361843 mm maximum includes local wall deformation and axial translation. Gravity equilibrium uses mesh volume; Euler uses exact CAD section and mass.
The pin ended Euler compression screen gives PE = 333,747.9 N and Pallow = 32,021.3 N. The 10 N axial utilization is 0.0003123. This global compression screen uses the conservative section and span.
Bracket analytical screening
The end force envelope is R = 20 N. The sum F + Wbeam + Wbracket is 16.909 N, using a bounding volume for the bracket weight. Independent applied moment envelopes are T = 550 N mm about the tube axis and Mt = 1600 N mm transverse to it.
The required transverse moment bound is 1,536.461 N mm. The 6 mm tapered reinforcement and 2 mm root fillet spread the load at the plug junction; their size responds to the root stress concentration found during design. The FEM retains both features.
Chamfered plug section
With B = 31.55 mm and C = 3 mm, Ap = 977.403 mm² and Ip = 78,630.434 mm⁴. Bending uses the actual octagonal section. Shear and torsion retain a conservative inscribed square of side a = B − 2C = 25.550 mm.
Using projection ℓ = 32 mm gives a combined plug screen of 0.746 MPa against 14/3 = 4.667 MPa. The force plus end moment displacement estimate is 0.00943 mm.
Plate and interface screens
The plate strip estimate is 0.986 MPa. Two assumed 10 mm axial bearing patches in 30 mm engagement have centers 20 mm apart. Thus Fc = 20 + 1600/20 = 100 N and p = 100/255.5 = 0.391 MPa. These patches and the local wall strip are screening assumptions. Physical contact, interference crushing and sustained bearing remain unqualified; the fit ribs carry no credited structural load.
Wall anchors and installation load path
Two fischer 564572 anchors attach each plate to provisionally solid C20/25 concrete in a dry interior. Their centres are 120 mm apart. The project assumes at least 100 mm wall thickness and at least 60 mm to every free edge. Those conditions are unverified at the actual site [S8 S9].
An 8 mm EN1A sleeve carries installation preload through an 18.6 mm printed hole. A 30 mm retention washer spans the sleeve and print; keep the original fischer washer and nut above it. The original small washer alone cannot retain the printed plate. Each measured sleeve must stand at least 0.1 mm proud of the local flange [S10 S11].
The anchor load table gives permissible tension 700 N and shear 4300 N for an isolated M6 anchor in cracked C20/25. Its values include material factors and an action factor of 1.4. ETA characteristic bending 11 Nm with factor 1.25 gives a service screening reference of 6285.714 N mm [S8 S9].
Using e = 45.8 mm, s = 120 mm and d = 6 mm gives N = 40.967 N, V = 24.583 N and Mb = 2,833.125 N mm. One bolt is conservatively assigned the full direct load and full global bending moment because clearances and the vertical bolt alignment cannot guarantee equal sharing in every direction.
Combined screening utilization is 0.5150. This linear sum is a conservative project screen. It does not replace the full fastening design method or verify the custom fixture and concrete surface.
Tightening preload remains a qualification gate
The specified 8 Nm torque with assumed K = 0.15 to 0.25 gives 5,333 to 8,889 N. Minimum sleeve area is 210.10 mm² and its estimated mean compression is 42.308 MPa. K is not manufacturer preload data. Washer bending, local concrete bearing, installation damage and actual torque performance remain blocked even though external beam loads are small.

Finite element model and three dimensional bracket stress
FreeCAD creates solids; Gmsh generates STL and FEM meshes. CalculiX uses quadratic C3D10 displacement interpolation. Bracket geometry is curved second order; beam geometry is straight sided to avoid curved element inversion in the thin wall. Gmsh nodes 9 and 10 are remapped to CalculiX ordering. Raw decks, logs and results are retained.
Beam ends are restrained transversely with one axial fix; a central 10 N patch and gravity load the tube. Bracket tip cases are balanced resultants: fx/fy/fz at 20 N, tx at 550 N mm, my/mz at 1600 N mm. Structural geometry retains reinforcement and fillet but omits fit ribs and interference preload.
Figure 3  Bracket nodal stress field. The arbitrary-direction bound combines independent solutions and is not one physical stress tensor. PETG is isotropic in this screening model.
For the principal bound, each Si is maximum absolute principal stress, the spectral norm of its symmetric tensor. Triangle and Cauchy Schwarz inequalities give the bound; the same combination bounds von Mises stress. DAT shears are reordered from XY/XZ/YZ to XY/YZ/ZX before the eigenvalue calculation. Both nodal and integration point results are checked.

Manufacture assembly and physical acceptance
First resolve both numerical failures in the next version: refine the joint/contact mesh at the washer restraint edge and improve the tube curvature/geometry approximation. Then obtain measured print, fit, wall and assembly evidence. The proposed physical qualification sequence follows. Site drilling and torque installation require the actual substrate and manufacturer procedure to be confirmed.
1. Inspect the purchased tube and material documentation. Finish cut to 400 ±0.5 mm, deburr, and record both inside dimensions and corner clearance at both ends. Verify the actual wall bearing face spacing against the CAD stack.
2. Print the appropriate fit coupons with the proposed PETG process. Select or regenerate a coupon from measured stock rather than assuming nominal interference. Record fit force, seating, damage and retention. Use the same qualified process for both brackets.
3. Inspect each printed flange, plug root, rib and sleeve bore. Confirm that the metal sleeves fit the bores and each stands at least 0.1 mm proud of the local printed surface. Keep the wide washer and original anchor washer and nut in the specified order.
4. Qualify the actual wall and installation. Check concrete class, wall thickness, every edge distance, anchor pitch, buried services and absence of weak finishes under the sleeves. Follow the current M6 ETA drilling, cleaning and 8 Nm torque procedure. Examine the metal and concrete bearing path.
5. Preassemble both plugs and the tube, then position the assembly between the prepared walls with bolt access above and below the beam. Do not force an assembly into an undersized gap or pry the printed flanges into alignment.
6. After installation qualification, use an agreed controlled proof procedure for the assembled load path. A proposed 30 N proof level requires review of the complete fixture, wall and test setup; the material screening factor alone does not qualify that test. Record direction, point, duration, deflection, residual movement and inspection outcome.

Release decision

Physical qualification is BLOCKED for fit, printed material, contact, installation, actual walls, duration and environment.
